Licences

CREW Client's original work is under the MIT licence (opens in a new tab). The fonts are Geist and Geist Mono, under the SIL Open Font License (opens in a new tab). Every asset on this site is listed in LEGAL.md.

Privacy

This site sets no cookies, has no analytics or trackers and loads nothing from third parties except GitHub. When you open a release, the server keeps a one-way hash of your IP address so that release counts each visitor once; the address itself is never stored. To show the latest releases, patch notes, links and contributors, your browser asks api.github.com and raw.githubusercontent.com for them, and avatars.githubusercontent.com for the contributors' pictures; GitHub's privacy statement (opens in a new tab) covers those requests. The launcher sends nothing unless you allow it: what it can send, and how to delete it (opens in a new tab).

Performance

CREW Client is built to run Minecraft with less work: less memory, no pauses from a Java runtime, and a renderer that keeps the graphics card busy. This page says what CREW Client does differently, and what has been measured. Where nothing has been published yet, it says so.

On this page

  • The client
  • Servers
  • Where else to look

What CREW Client does differently

  • Native code. CREW Client is Rust, compiled for your system. There is no Java virtual machine, so no garbage-collection pauses and no warm-up while the code gets compiled as you play.
  • It ticks what vanilla ticks, and no more. Mobs, minecarts and dropped items outside the simulation distance only check whether to despawn, as in vanilla. Before this was fixed, CREW Client ticked seven times as many of them as vanilla at render distance 32.
  • Work off the main thread. Chunks are generated, lit and meshed on worker threads, and unloaded chunks are written to disk in the background.
  • Fewer, bigger draw calls. Terrain sections share one vertex arena and are drawn with one indirect multi-draw per pass. Mobs and block entities outside the view are skipped, and caves you cannot see into are culled.
  • Distant terrain. The optional LOD distance draws terrain up to 1024 chunks away without simulating it.
  • A server without Java. horizon-server is one native program. See below.

The client

The numbers below are CREW Client before and after a round of performance work, on an RTX 4080, in a fresh creative world flown over a village, a forest, the ocean and a mountain (tools/graphics_bench.py). They compare CREW Client with an earlier CREW Client, not with vanilla.

  • Render distance 8 over a village: 363 to 2,409 FPS, and the slowest 1% of frames from 21.6 to 160.8 FPS.
  • Render distance 16 over a village: 8 to 1,041 FPS. In a forest: 28 to 316 FPS.
  • Render distance 32 improved too (a village went from 4 to 48 FPS) but still stutters in places: the slow frames are game ticks while chunks load, not drawing.

The full tables, with the method and the Linux software-rendering run, are in docs/graphics.md.

CREW Client against vanilla. The benchmark exists: tools/perf_bench.py starts CREW Client, vanilla 26.3 and vanilla with Sodium, Lithium, FerriteCore and ImmediatelyFast on the same machine, world, camera path and settings, and computes every number the same way. docs/benchmark.md says how it keeps the comparison fair. Its results for the client, measured on the project's own machines, are on the Benchmarks page, with the places where Java is faster. It also estimates other hardware from them, as a range, and shows players' uploaded results once at least three installs share a graphics card and processor.

Measure your own computer. CREW Client's half of that benchmark is built into the game. Close other heavy apps, plug in a laptop, and run in a terminal:

horizon-launcher play --arg=--benchmark --arg=standard

Add --arg=--render-distance --arg=16 to choose the render distance; your other settings are used as they are and stored in the report. The game creates its own world, horizon-benchmark (it never touches your saves), flies over a village, a forest, the ocean and a mountain, and quits by itself. --arg=village (or forest, ocean, mountain) instead of --arg=standard flies one scene. For the run only, VSync is off and the frame rate unlimited; your options are not changed.

The report is a .json file in the benchmarks folder of the game folder:

SystemFolder
Windows%APPDATA%\horizon\benchmarks
macOS~/Library/Application Support/horizon/benchmarks
Linux~/.local/share/horizon/benchmarks

It holds startup times, each scene's chunk load time, the frame rate with its 1% and 0.1% lows, memory, and your machine (operating system, processor, memory, graphics card and driver). Its format is docs/benchmark-report.md.

Share it. To add your computer to the Benchmarks page, either:

  • upload it from the launcher: horizon-launcher data benchmark <the report's path> shows exactly what would be sent, without your file paths, and asks before sending. Or allow benchmark uploads once (horizon-launcher data allow benchmarks), and every later report is sent for you. See Privacy;
  • or post the .json file in the Discord.

Servers

The latest bot benchmark compares horizon-server with vanilla 26.3, Paper and vanilla on Fabric with Lithium. It ran on a 4-thread laptop CPU (an i7-7500U), with 1 to 50 bots walking, exploring and standing around spawn, on another machine. All four servers did the same work. In short:

  • Memory: 0.7 GB with one player and 1.1 GB with 50, against 3.4 to 4.5 GB for the Java servers (each with a 4 GB heap). That is 3.8 to 4.8 times less.
  • Start: ready in 8 to 9 seconds, against 15 for vanilla, 17 for Fabric and 19 to 22 for Paper.
  • CPU: from 10 players up, the least of the four.
  • Average tick: Paper's was shorter at every player count, and Fabric with Lithium's at 1 and 10 players. CREW Client's was shorter than vanilla's, and than Fabric's from 25 players up.
  • Slow ticks: from 10 players up, CREW Client's slowest ticks were the shortest of the four.
  • 50 players: CREW Client and Paper kept 20 TPS. Fabric with Lithium dropped to 17, and vanilla's watchdog stopped it.

For more than 50 active players, give it more cores. The full report is benchmarks/server-four-way-2026-10-19.
